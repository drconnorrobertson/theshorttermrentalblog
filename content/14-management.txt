What does a rental property manager do?|A manager's role depends on the contract. It may include guest communication, calendar management, cleaning coordination, maintenance, reporting, and other services. Some tasks may remain with the owner or incur extra charges. Do not infer the scope from the title alone. Compare written responsibilities with the work the property actually needs, including urgent support and verification of readiness.|Map every recurring task to the manager, owner, or another provider.
How is a co-host different from a manager?|Co-host describes an operating role that can vary by platform and agreement, while management is a broader service arrangement. Either may handle a few tasks or substantial operations. Permissions, payment, responsibility, and access should be defined explicitly. Informal terminology does not resolve contractual or legal obligations. Compare the actual scope and authority rather than assuming one title is cheaper or more comprehensive.|Write a task-and-authority schedule for the proposed arrangement.
How do I evaluate a management contract?|Review scope, fee basis, additional charges, authority, reporting, account access, termination, data handling, and responsibilities for existing reservations. Check exclusions as carefully as included services. Ask for worked examples and have significant legal questions reviewed appropriately. A headline fee percentage is not a complete comparison. The contract should describe how ordinary work and exceptions are handled.|Create a list of unclear provisions before committing.
What should I ask a potential co-host?|Ask about relevant experience, availability, specific tasks, response process, backup support, reporting, fees, and handling of sensitive access. Request evidence rather than assuming past success transfers to your property. Discuss difficult scenarios such as an entry failure or cleaner cancellation. Clarify platform permissions and contract responsibilities. A good fit connects the person's capability with the actual operating needs.|Walk through three realistic incidents and ask who does what.
How do I compare management fees?|Compare the total cost for the same scope and reservation pattern. Check which revenue categories form the fee basis, along with minimums, maintenance charges, onboarding, marketing, and exit costs. A low percentage may exclude essential services. Evaluate the work you must still arrange and its cost. Keep the comparison written so differences are visible.|Request a representative monthly invoice under each proposal.
Who should control the booking accounts?|Control and access should follow platform rules, contracts, and the actual ownership arrangement. Clarify who owns listings, receives payments, changes settings, and retains records if the relationship ends. Avoid sharing credentials informally when role-based access is available. Account control can affect continuity and handover. Get appropriate advice where rights are unclear rather than treating technical access as proof of ownership.|Document account roles and the termination handover process.
How do I supervise a manager remotely?|Use agreed reporting, readiness verification, incident records, expense approvals, and periodic reviews. Focus on evidence of outcomes rather than constant messages. Define escalation and backup responsibilities. A manager's summary should reconcile with financial and booking records. Remote oversight works best when the owner knows what to review and the manager knows what decisions require approval.|Create a regular report with exceptions and unresolved tasks highlighted.
What should a monthly management report include?|Include completed stays, realized revenue, payouts, relevant expenses, maintenance, guest issues, and upcoming operational concerns. State definitions and distinguish earned revenue from future bookings. Reconcile charges with supporting records. The report should identify decisions needed from the owner rather than simply display a positive headline number. Consistency allows useful comparisons over time.|Agree a report format and reconcile one sample month before launch.
How should repair spending authority work?|Define routine thresholds, urgent exceptions, required evidence, and notification timing. Clarify whether contractor markups or coordination charges apply. The process needs to allow timely action without creating unlimited informal authority. Record each material decision and verify completion. Review thresholds as the property or portfolio changes. A clear authority schedule reduces both delays and unexpected charges.|Attach spending limits and emergency procedures to the management arrangement.
What should I check before switching managers?|Review termination terms, future reservations, account access, guest communications, contractor arrangements, inventory, payments, and records. Plan the transition around service continuity. A manager change does not automatically transfer listings or permissions. Confirm responsibilities during the handover period and reconcile funds. Seek appropriate advice on disputed contractual provisions. The property should remain understandable to guests while internal roles change.|Build a dated handover checklist with both outgoing and incoming responsibilities.
How do I handle overlapping responsibilities?|Identify the task, one accountable owner, supporting roles, and the point where a handoff occurs. Overlap can cause duplication or leave everyone assuming another person acted. Use one authoritative schedule and record completion. Clarify exceptions such as urgent repairs or guest compensation. A task matrix should reflect actual practice rather than sit unused in a contract folder.|Assign one final decision-maker for each critical task.
Should a manager guarantee revenue?|Treat a claimed guarantee as a contractual provision requiring careful evaluation, not proof of future performance. Ask about definitions, exclusions, conditions, duration, payment responsibility, and remedies. Review it with appropriate professional advice. Historical results and estimates are different from enforceable commitments. Model the property independently and avoid relying on promotional wording to replace downside planning.|Request the exact written terms and assess the base case without the guarantee.
How do I evaluate manager performance?|Use service outcomes, accurate reporting, expense control, communication, guest issues, and performance against consistently defined assumptions. Separate market changes from execution failures. A manager can influence operations without controlling every source of demand. Review evidence and corrective actions rather than focusing only on one rating or revenue figure. The evaluation should connect each concern to a task the manager actually owns.|Agree measurable responsibilities and review them with supporting records.
What is a management onboarding checklist?|It records the property setup, permissions, inventory, access, listings, calendar, contractor arrangements, communication templates, and financial reporting process. It also identifies unresolved issues and the decision to accept bookings. Onboarding should include verification rather than simply receiving documents. Keep the checklist current if responsibilities change. A structured start reduces confusion during the first guest incident.|Run a readiness walkthrough with the people responsible for operations.
How should I evaluate a local manager's availability?|Ask who responds during routine hours, overnight incidents, holidays, and absences. Verify local response capability and backups. A promise of availability should describe what action can actually be taken, not merely whether someone reads a message. Consider travel time and the tasks requiring physical attendance. Test the contact process before relying on it for a guest-facing commitment.|Document coverage hours, response roles, and backup contacts.
What should a manager disclose about contractors?|Clarify contractor selection, qualifications where relevant, charges, coordination fees, related-party arrangements, and expense approval. The owner should understand how work is authorized and verified. Contractor use can be efficient, but opaque arrangements make cost and quality difficult to assess. Appropriate insurance and legal questions depend on the working structure. Request a transparent process rather than assuming every vendor is independent.|Ask for the procurement and approval procedure in writing.
How do I keep owner and manager communication efficient?|Use a regular review cadence, defined urgent channels, and a shared record of tasks and decisions. Separate routine updates from issues needing immediate approval. Too many scattered messages can obscure commitments. Agree the information required for a decision so the manager does not repeatedly seek clarification. Efficiency comes from clear responsibility and accessible evidence, not from eliminating oversight.|Use a decision log and an exception-focused regular report.
Can I manage one property while outsourcing selected tasks?|Yes, a limited service arrangement may suit an operation if the responsibilities are clear and lawful. You can coordinate cleaning, maintenance, or guest messaging separately, but someone must manage the handoffs and verify results. Partial outsourcing can reduce one workload while increasing coordination. Compare total cost and time with broader management options. Avoid leaving urgent or out-of-hours tasks unassigned.|Create a complete task matrix before contracting individual services.
What should I ask about management termination fees?|Ask when fees apply, how they are calculated, and what obligations continue after termination. Review notice periods, existing reservations, data, account access, and contractor commitments. These provisions can materially affect a future switch. A management proposal that is attractive initially may be costly to exit. Have unclear legal terms reviewed before signing rather than discovering their effect during a dispute.|Calculate a sample exit under the proposed contract.
How do I handle a disagreement with a manager?|Document the issue, supporting records, relevant responsibility, and the remedy requested. Use the contract's communication and dispute process, with appropriate advice for significant matters. Distinguish operational misunderstandings from financial or legal disputes. Protect service continuity for guests while resolving the relationship. Factual records make the discussion more useful than broad accusations about effort or intentions.|Create a timeline and identify the specific provision or task at issue.
Who should handle guest refunds in a managed property?|Authority should be defined in the agreement and aligned with current booking rules and obligations. Clarify thresholds, documentation, approval, and who bears the cost. A manager needs enough authority to respond appropriately without making unexplained commitments. Keep guest communication consistent with the decision. Review material incidents afterward to improve both service and governance.|Document refund authority and the evidence required for each decision.
How do I protect business data during a handover?|Identify relevant records, lawful access, retention duties, and the systems holding them. Transfer necessary information through secure approved methods and remove obsolete access through the proper process. Guest privacy and platform rules still apply. Avoid broad credential sharing as a shortcut. The handover should preserve operational continuity without copying unnecessary personal information or leaving former providers with ongoing permissions.|Create a role-based access and record-transfer checklist.
How should I evaluate a manager's case study?|Ask what property, period, revenue definition, costs, and operating conditions the example describes. Check whether evidence is available and whether the property resembles yours. A strong result in one situation does not predict your outcome. Testimonials and marketing summaries may omit limitations. Use case studies to identify questions and capabilities, then evaluate your property with its own assumptions.|Request underlying definitions and distinguish verified records from promotional statements.
What responsibilities remain with an owner after outsourcing?|The answer depends on the arrangement, but ownership obligations, strategic decisions, cash funding, and oversight often remain relevant. Confirm legal, tax, financing, and insurance duties with appropriate advisers. A management contract does not automatically eliminate every responsibility. Identify what requires owner approval and what records the owner must retain. Outsourcing is a division of work that needs supervision.|List retained duties explicitly beside the manager's scope.
When should I reconsider my management arrangement?|Review it when service failures recur, reporting becomes unreliable, costs diverge from agreed terms, or the property needs work outside the provider's scope. Also reassess after portfolio or personal circumstances change. Start with specific evidence and possible corrections rather than switching impulsively. A transition itself creates costs and responsibilities. Compare the current arrangement with realistic alternatives and a workable handover plan.|Set review triggers and document the decision process.
